# Product & Marketing Copywriting

Skill ID: `content-copywriting`
Library: `software-planning-lead-v5.6.1`
Version: `5.6.1`

## Purpose

Create differentiated, truthful, audience-specific website/product copy that communicates value clearly and supports user action without fabricated proof or manipulative tactics.

## Profiles

marketing_website, content_website, ecommerce

## Typical roles

content-writer, content-strategist, product-marketer

## Inputs
- primary audience and awareness level;
- actual product capability/differentiators;
- proof available (customer evidence, data, demos, credentials);
- page intent and desired action;
- brand voice/terms;
- legal/compliance constraints.

## Core principles
Specific > clever. Evidence > adjectives. One audience/action per page section. Write from verified product facts; placeholders are better than invented proof.

## Message architecture
Before drafting, define:
1. audience;
2. situation/problem;
3. desired outcome;
4. differentiated mechanism/approach;
5. proof available;
6. primary CTA;
7. objections.

Choose a framework only as scaffolding (problem-solution, before-after, feature→benefit, narrative). Do not expose formulaic copy.

## Page workflow
### Hero
Communicate what it is, who it helps and why it matters quickly. Headline can be brand-led or benefit-led; clarity is mandatory. Supporting copy adds mechanism/context. CTA says what happens next.

### Proof
Place evidence adjacent to claims. Valid proof includes real product screenshots, verified results, named customer quotes with permission, case-study data, certifications actually held, transparent methodology.

### Feature/benefit sections
Connect capability → user consequence → evidence/example. Avoid repeating the same benefit with synonyms.

### Objections/FAQ
Answer real barriers honestly: setup, migration, compatibility, pricing model, security, support, cancellation, limitations. FAQ is not a dumping ground for SEO keywords.

### Product microcopy
Buttons, labels, empty states and errors should be concise, concrete and consistent with UX behavior. Destructive confirmations name the consequence.

## Voice
Define 3-5 voice traits plus "not this" contrasts. Example: authoritative but not corporate; playful but not flippant. Vary sentence rhythm naturally. Remove filler transitions, empty superlatives and AI cliches.

## Truthfulness rules
Never invent:
- testimonials/customers/logos;
- revenue/conversion/user counts;
- awards/certifications;
- benchmark superiority;
- prices/discounts;
- scarcity/deadlines;
- security/compliance status.

When fact missing, mark `[NEEDS VERIFIED FACT: ...]`.

## SEO alignment
Human usefulness leads. Incorporate topic/entity language naturally, descriptive headings and internal links; do not force exact-match phrases. `seo-content` owns technical/crawl/schema decisions.

## Anti-patterns
- "revolutionary", "game-changing", "seamless" without evidence;
- vague headlines that could describe competitors;
- feature lists with no outcome;
- fake urgency/social proof;
- one generic CTA repeated regardless of context;
- paragraphs all same length/structure;
- writing for everyone;
- burying limitations users need to decide.

## Review checklist
- Can target user identify relevance within first screen?
- Is every material claim verifiable?
- Does each section add new information?
- Are headings meaningful out of context?
- Is CTA outcome explicit?
- Are objections answered rather than spun?
- Does the copy sound like one coherent brand/person, not generic AI?

## Evidence
Maintain claim source/placeholders where material. Final public copy has no invented facts and aligns with implemented behavior/pricing/policies.

## V5.6.1 Product Truth and System Copy

Treat copy as part of the product contract. Distinguish marketing claims, UI labels, validation/error messages, onboarding guidance, transactional communication and legal/compliance text. Never invent prices, certifications, guarantees, customer quotes, feature availability or policy claims.

For product UI, optimize for action clarity: button labels name the action, errors explain recovery, destructive confirmations name the consequence, empty states explain the next useful step, and jargon is introduced only when the audience uses it. Maintain terminology consistency across UI, docs and API-visible concepts.

For localized products, avoid layouts/copy that depend on English word length and record strings whose meaning cannot be safely translated without product context.

## V5.7.1 Planning participation
When user-facing content is in scope, participate during planning instead of leaving copy to the coding agent. Decide content mode, create research-backed briefs/copy where requested, and persist approved content under `planning/content/`. Read `references/authoritative-content-planning.md` for the authority and review pipeline.
